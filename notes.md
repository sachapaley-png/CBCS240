Skill: Excel for Financial Analysis

Why this skill: I want to pursue a career in the financial sector. Excel is the tool used daily in areas of banking, investment, and business analysis. By learning Excel my semester, I hope to build on my knowledge of Business Administration and Economics and prepare for internship opportunities and advanced skills in financial modeling.

The fundamentals of Excel.
Learn the basics of formulas, the SUM/AVERAGE/IF functions along with sorting, filtering, and some keyboard shortcuts by enrolling on one of the free Excel courses created by the Corporate Finance Institute or ExcelJet.
Useful options.
Find out how to use the functions XLOOKUP and SUMIF/COUNTIF as well as building charts by applying them to your own budget or tennis practice schedule.
Reading financial statements made simple.
Learn the contents of the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement; check out the CFI “Reading Financial Statements” free course and examine a company you like (like Nike).
Basic analysis.
Input the company's sales and profitability figures for three years into Excel, calculate growth rates and margins, and present them in the form of charts. 
Conclusion. Write a half-page paper on what the data is telling you.
Add “Excel (financial analysis)” to your CV as well as the description of the project completed.
